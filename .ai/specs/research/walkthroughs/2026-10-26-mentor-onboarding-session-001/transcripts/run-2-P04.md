## Decision simulation

**Base situation (State 01 invitation, one bookable session within two weeks):** I **ignore** it. Cold invite from people I don't know, a two-week ask, and nobody I know is going to book me first. Without that first known booking I won't publish availability, so accepting would just create an account I'd forget. If something nudged me to click, the most I'd do is accept but publish nothing.

- **P1 Two weeks pass with no booking.** Doesn't flip — reinforces. I either never accepted, or I accepted and forgot. A quiet month after joining is exactly what kills it for me.
- **P2 Public list by stack tag, next to strangers.** Doesn't flip — makes the "no" firmer. Being listed next to people I don't know is what I object to. I want to be found only by people I send the link to; there's no option to stay off the list.
- **P3 Prices $20–$200, platform keeps 20%.** Doesn't flip. Money isn't my lever. If anything, $200 as a ceiling is fine; the 20% doesn't bother me. Price is not why I say no.
- **P4 Instant booking, question only visible when session starts.** Doesn't flip because I'm already at no — but this is the one that would turn a "maybe" into a hard no. Question up front is my condition. Seeing it when the session starts means I committed blind.
- **P5 Stripe Connect before first payout.** Doesn't flip. Bank details aren't the issue; it's a chore I'd do only after someone actually booked. I don't know my employer's stance on side income — that might matter, I simply don't know.

**Where the story and the choice part ways:** In the interview I said I like answering concrete questions in text, between meetings — which is precisely what this product offers. Yet I still ignore it. The gap is: the product wants me to publish availability first and be booked by strangers, while my real behaviour is to answer someone I chose, after seeing the question. The format fits; the entry doesn't.
