- **Fast reaction:** Same as always: someone asked something I've answered five times, and I went to my notes.
- **Feeling:** mild amusement mixed with mild weariness; trigger: the repeat.
- **Considered answer:** Someone opened an issue on my repository that was really a "how do I" question, not a bug. ### Q2 What did that cost you — time, money, risk? What did you try before?

Before, I tried a paid call with someone once; it was transactional: we talked, they left, and nothing survived the call. No write-up, nothing I could point the next person to. That felt worse than answering for free in an issue, because at least the issue stays.
- **Grounded in:** "two evenings a month, for free, and the notes rot."; "transactional, nothing survives the call."

## Decision simulation

**What I do with the invitation (State 01):** accept and publish one slot. I'm curious, and the page lets me point at my repository instead of a rating, so I fill it in one evening, set prices, publish a single evening slot. I do **not** share the link with my audience. I want to see whether the platform itself brings anyone before I send my people through a place that keeps 20%.

- **P1 Two weeks pass with no booking.** Flips it, but quietly: I let the slot lapse and don't publish another. That's the answer to my test: the platform brought no audience. I wouldn't delete the account, I'd just stop.
- **P2 Public list filtered by stack tag, next to people I don't know.** Doesn't flip. That list is the only thing that could bring people I don't already reach, so it's the point. As long as it's ordered without scores, sitting next to strangers is fine. My GitHub speaks for me.
- **P3 Prices $20–$200 set by the operator; platform keeps 20%.** Doesn't flip the single slot; I don't know what to charge or whether my audience would pay. But it hardens my choice not to share the link. If the mentee came through my own link, 20% is a cut on an audience I built.
- **P4 Instant booking; I see the question only when the session starts.** Leans toward flipping. I prepare answers from my notes; walking in blind means answering live, which is exactly the call-shaped thing I didn't like. I'd keep the one slot but set it to the 25-minute length only, and I'd want the question at booking.
- **P5 Stripe (bank details) before the first payout.** Doesn't flip. The screen says I can be booked before connecting, so I'd only connect if someone actually books.

**Where the interview and the choice part ways:** In the interview I said the problem is gone only when my notes earn without me on a call and stay under my name. What I actually do is publish a live, timed session that leaves only a private note: the transactional shape I said I disliked. I accept anyway out of curiosity, as a test of whether the list brings people, not because it solves my problem. Also: I said I point people to my own resources, but here I deliberately don't share my link.
